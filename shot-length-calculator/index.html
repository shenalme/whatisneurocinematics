What average shot length tells you

Average shot length (ASL) is the running time divided by the number of shots. It is the most widely used measure of editing pace in film studies. A low ASL means rapid cutting; a high ASL means longer, more contemplative takes.

Why the median matters too

A single long take can inflate the average. The median shot length is less affected by outliers, so film scholars often report both. Paste cut times above to see both values and the full distribution.

How editing pace has changed

Research by James Cutting and colleagues (2010, Psychological Science) found that shots in popular Hollywood films have become steadily shorter over the decades, from around ten seconds in the 1930s to around four seconds in the 2000s. They linked this to rhythms that may fit the natural fluctuations of human attention.

How to count shots

  • Scrub through the scene and note the timestamp of every cut, including fast dissolves.
  • Free shot boundary detection tools such as PySceneDetect can find cuts automatically; check the results by eye.
  • Paste the timestamps here in seconds (93.5) or minutes and seconds (1:33.5).
